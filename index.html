Pere Marquette River · Branch, Michigan

A few good days on the river each fall.

Come float the Pere Marquette with a retired local who loves this river. We row a drift boat through quiet, colorful water in search of fall Chinook salmon. It's a small, unhurried outing for two anglers, with lunch on us.

The Outing

One day, one river, a shot at a king

This isn't a big charter operation. It's a retired guide spending a few fall days a year doing what he loves: rowing the boat, reading the water and putting two anglers on fresh Chinook salmon.

The season

  • The peak of the fall run: from the second Tuesday of September through the third Thursday of October.
  • Full days on Tuesdays and Thursdays only, 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM.
  • Booking opens July 1 each year. Booking for the 2027 season opens July 1.
  • Payment is made in person on the day. Nothing is charged online.

Your guide provides the gear

  • Lamiglas rods and Daiwa reels
  • Fresh cured spawn and spawn bags, beads and stick baits
  • A large river landing net
  • A full lunch on the river

You bring your own waders (strictly no cleats or studded soles in the boat) and a valid Michigan fishing license. Full gear guide →

The Float

Upper Branch to Lower Branch

We put in at the Upper Branch Access and take out at the Lower Branch Access, both in Branch, Michigan. In between is a full day of quiet, tree-lined water on the Pere Marquette River.

An illustrated sketch of the float, not a survey map. Features shift with the water, but the spirit is the same. See the Upper Branch access on a map · Lower Branch access

  1. 1Put-in, Upper Branch Access
    We load the boat, say good morning to the river and push off at 8:00 AM.
  2. 2The first bend
    Quiet, secluded water under a canopy of fall color. A good place to settle in.
  3. 3Log jam run
    Fallen timber makes classic holding water. We drift it carefully and watch for a take.
  4. 4Gravel riffle
    Moving water over clean gravel where salmon like to rest and run.
  5. 5Deep holding pool
    The kind of slow, deep pool where king salmon stack up.
  6. 6Hardwood bend
    Maples and birches in full color. Keep the camera handy.
  7. 7Take-out, Lower Branch Access
    We land the boat around 5:00 PM, tired and happy, with stories to tell.

Meet Your Guide

A retired local, rowing for the fun of it

This is a labor of love, not a business empire. Your guide rows the boat himself, knows this stretch of the Pere Marquette and is out here a few days each fall because it's the best place he knows to be.

Expect friendly conversation, honest advice and plenty of patience, whether you're a seasoned salmon angler or it's your very first drift boat day.
